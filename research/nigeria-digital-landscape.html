Nigeria Digital Statistics 2026: Internet, Search and Social Media Data

By Sadiq Dantanko, Founder of Dala Forge. Published September 2026. Updated as new official data is released.

Direct answer: Nigeria has 157.4 million active internet subscribers, Google holds roughly 98.7% of all search traffic — one of the highest concentrations of any country measured — and WhatsApp is used by 96.5% of internet users. The first two numbers are where AEO work actually happens. The third explains why the gap it closes is unusually wide here: most Nigerian business trust is built inside a closed channel AI systems can't see at all, which raises the stakes for the parts of a business's presence that are open and citable, not a reason to treat WhatsApp itself as an AEO channel.

This page exists as a standing reference: sourced, dated figures on Nigeria's connectivity, search, and social media landscape, kept in one place instead of scattered across our other reports. Every figure below is dated and sourced — the same evidence standard we apply to our own client work.

How many Nigerians are actually online?

Direct answer: 157.4 million active internet subscribers as of May 2026, according to the Nigerian Communications Commission (NCC) — up from 148.2 million in December 2025. Broadband penetration reached 56.11%, and total telecoms subscriptions across all networks stood at over 185 million. Separately, NCC data shows 83.2% of Nigerian phone users are now connected to the internet in some form, meaning mobile-first is no longer a caveat for Nigerian digital strategy — it is close to the entire addressable audience.

Is Google still the default search engine in Nigeria, or has AI search changed that?

Direct answer: Google's dominance in Nigeria is, if anything, more extreme than the global average. StatCounter data for 2026 puts Google's all-device search market share in Nigeria at approximately 98.7%, placing it among the highest national concentrations of Google search usage measured anywhere — ahead of the United States (85.05%), well ahead of the global average (roughly 90%), and comparable to markets like Iran, Iraq and Thailand. Alternative search engines have essentially no practical presence in Nigeria.

The implication for AEO is the opposite of what that number might first suggest. Total dependence on one search provider doesn't make AI-mediated visibility less important — it makes it more concentrated. As Google increasingly answers queries directly through AI Overviews and AI-generated summaries rather than a list of blue links, the businesses that shape what those Google-generated answers say about them are the ones with something to gain from Nigeria's near-total Google dependence, not something to lose. This is the exact mechanism our report on AI-driven web traffic documents in more depth, and our report on what actually gets cited by AI systems shows why ranking well in that 98.7% doesn't automatically mean being cited in the AI answers sitting above it.

Which platform actually matters most for a Nigerian business beyond Google?

Direct answer: WhatsApp, by a wide margin. A 2026 social media data report found that 96.5% of Nigerian internet users aged 16 and older use WhatsApp at least once a month — the highest usage figure of any platform measured — and it is the self-declared favourite platform for 33.9% of users, ahead of Facebook (29.7%) and TikTok (15.7%). TikTok is the fastest-growing platform by advertising reach, up 43.4% year-over-year to 47.8 million, while Facebook remains a large, stable base at 38.0 million.

The practical read is the opposite of "manage your WhatsApp presence." WhatsApp's dominance means the large majority of Nigerian business activity — referrals, group recommendations, catalog browsing, direct orders — happens inside a closed, private channel that search engines and AI systems cannot read, index or cite. A business can have genuine trust and volume moving through WhatsApp and still show up to an AI system as unverified, because none of that activity leaves a public trail. This is precisely why the evidence gap AEO work closes is wider in Nigeria than in more desktop-and-open-web-oriented markets: real reputation is disproportionately trapped somewhere AI can't see it, which raises the value of building citable evidence elsewhere — not a reason to try to make WhatsApp itself legible to AI, which isn't realistic and isn't what Dala Forge does. Our homepage FAQ addresses this directly for businesses that run mostly on WhatsApp and referrals.

What does this mean for how a Nigerian business should approach AEO?

Direct answer: Two things follow directly from the numbers above, and both point toward the same conclusion: the gap between real business strength and AI-visible evidence is unusually wide in Nigeria specifically. First, mobile-first isn't optional design guidance here — at 83.2% phone-to-internet conversion, it's close to the only real audience, so anything meant to be AI-citable has to actually work on that audience's terms. Second, and more importantly, WhatsApp's near-total dominance (96.5% usage) means the channel where most Nigerian business trust actually gets built is structurally invisible to the AI systems this whole report is about — closed, unindexed, uncitable. That isn't a WhatsApp problem to fix; it's the specific reason the open, indexable side of a business's presence — its website, directories, structured data, independently verifiable evidence — carries more weight in Nigeria than it might elsewhere, because it's often the *only* part of a real, trusted business an AI system can actually see.

Digital Authority: What Google's E-E-A-T Framework Actually Measures

By Sadiq Dantanko, Founder of Dala Forge. Published September 2026.

Direct answer: Digital authority is not a score a business assigns itself — it's Google's term, in essence, for being independently recognised rather than self-promoted. The formal framework behind this is E-E-A-T: Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness and Trustworthiness, drawn from Google's own 176-page Search Quality Rater Guidelines. Google states plainly that E-E-A-T isn't a direct ranking factor — but the signals it describes measurably correlate with both search rankings and AI citation, and the gap between businesses that build real authority and those that only claim it is widening, not narrowing.

Every figure below comes from a named, dated source — the same evidence standard we apply to our own client work.

What does E-E-A-T actually stand for, and where does it come from?

Direct answer: Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness and Trustworthiness — a framework defined in Google's Search Quality Rater Guidelines, a 176-page document given to the thousands of human raters Google uses to manually evaluate search result quality. The framework began as E-A-T in 2014; Google added the first "E," for Experience, in December 2022, recognising that genuine first-hand experience with a topic is a distinct signal from formal expertise. Of the four components, Google has stated that trust is the most important — the others contribute to trust, but content doesn't need to demonstrate all four to be considered high-quality.

Is E-E-A-T actually a Google ranking factor?

Direct answer: No, and Google is explicit about this — there is no single "E-E-A-T score" built into the algorithm. What E-E-A-T actually is: a framework describing the qualities Google's automated systems and human quality raters are designed to reward. The practical distinction doesn't change much for a business, because the individual signals E-E-A-T describes — author credentials, backlinks from credible sites, consistent accuracy, positive independent reviews — are the things Google's ranking systems do measure directly, and those same signals are documented as correlating with AI Overview citation as well as organic rank.

The scale of what these signals now affect is significant. Google's March 2026 Core Update — described as the most volatile update measured to date — moved 79.5% of Top-3 search positions, according to SE Ranking's analysis. An update of that size doesn't reshuffle rankings randomly; it re-weights toward the sites already demonstrating the underlying E-E-A-T signals most strongly.

Does original evidence actually outperform derivative content?

Direct answer: Yes, and the gap is large. Following the March 2026 Core Update, SE Ranking found that sites publishing original data gained 22% visibility on average — while AI-paraphrased content, reworded from other sources rather than built on original evidence, lost 71% of its traffic. This is close to the sharpest, most direct evidence available that authority genuinely means something distinct from simply publishing content: the sites rewarded were the ones contributing something new, not the ones repackaging what already existed elsewhere.

What actually builds digital authority?

Direct answer: Recognition by others, not self-description. One 2026 industry guide put it plainly: authority is "about being recognized, not self-promoted" — it comes from consistent mentions, backlinks, and citations from other respected sources, not from a business's own claims about itself. On the technical side, named author attribution, Person schema markup, and verified business entity data are documented as improving AI citation rates alongside conventional Google rankings — which is exactly the mechanism our Entity Clarity research covers in more depth, and the same reason our own audit methodology treats "Independent Evidence" as a separate, measured dimension rather than assuming a business's own claims are sufficient.

What does this mean for a Nigerian business specifically?

Direct answer: The same authority signals apply, but the starting point is often further back. Our Entity Clarity research found more than 17 million small Nigerian businesses operate without CAC registration — meaning for a large share of businesses, there isn't yet a single, stable legal identity for authority signals like backlinks and citations to attach to. Digital authority compounds on top of a clear entity; it can't substitute for one.

AI Adoption in Nigeria: What the Data Actually Shows

By Sadiq Dantanko, Founder of Dala Forge. Published September 2026.

Direct answer: Nigeria has the highest self-reported AI adoption rate of any country measured — 88% of adults, per a Google/Ipsos study — yet most Nigerian businesses' own digital footprint has not caught up. The result is a widening gap: Nigerians are using AI to research and decide on more things than almost anyone else on earth, while the businesses they are researching often remain thin or inconsistent in the exact places those AI systems look for evidence.

The consumer-side numbers are unambiguous. A Google/Ipsos study covered by Condia in early 2026 found that 88% of Nigerian adults had used an AI chatbot in the previous year, an 18-point jump from the year before, against a global average of 62%. The same report found 93% of Nigerians now use AI to learn or understand complex topics, up from 81% in 2024 and well above the global figure of 74%, and 91% report using AI tools at work in some capacity. A separate figure reported by PM News put the generative-AI-specific number — people using platforms like ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude directly, by name — at 84%.

Enthusiasm tracks the same pattern. A 2026 global AI usage report found Nigeria leading the world on AI enthusiasm, with 77% of online adults describing themselves as excited about the technology, compared with roughly a third of adults in the US and under 30% in the UK. This is not a market warming up to AI. It is a market where AI is already the default first step for a large share of everyday research and decision-making.

Business-side adoption is moving on a similar trajectory, if slightly behind consumer usage. Reporting by The Sun found that by July 2026, 74.1% of digitally enabled firms across Lagos, Abuja and Rivers were already using AI tools to automate operations and support growth. A September 2025 study by Arion Research, commissioned by Zoho, found that 93% of Nigerian organisations had begun some form of AI adoption, with 31% reaching advanced integration and 26.5% using AI across multiple departments — figures the study's authors described as ahead of several higher-income markets.

This adoption is happening on infrastructure that is real but still constrained. Nigerian Communications Commission data reported by THISDAY put active internet subscribers at 153.8 million as of March 2026, up from 141.7 million in January 2025, with mobile networks — not fixed broadband — carrying the overwhelming majority of that traffic. That same growth continued through a period of significant cost pressure: Tribune reported a roughly 50% tariff increase and data prices per gigabyte roughly doubling between late 2025 and early 2026, yet subscriber and usage figures kept climbing rather than falling. The practical implication for AEO specifically: AI usage in Nigeria is overwhelmingly mobile-first, which changes what "being found" actually requires compared with the desktop-oriented assumptions built into most AEO and SEO advice written for other markets. For the fuller, continuously-updated connectivity, search and social platform picture, see our Nigeria digital statistics reference.

Put together, these numbers describe a specific and unusual gap. In most markets people are told AI adoption, then digital business representation, then AI-mediated recommendation. In Nigeria, the first step has already happened at a scale most other countries have not reached — the constraint is the second one. A business can be genuinely well-regarded, well-established and growing, and still be functionally invisible to the exact tool the large majority of its own potential customers now use as a first step, simply because its digital footprint was never built with that tool in mind. The same structural shift shows up globally in our companion report on AI-driven web traffic, and the specific mechanism behind it — a business's own identity being unclear to the systems reading it — is broken down in our report on entity clarity. Nigeria is not an exception to this pattern, it is simply further along on the consumer-adoption side of it.
